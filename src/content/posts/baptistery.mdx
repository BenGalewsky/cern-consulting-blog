# LINAC4
I like to think of CERN as a medieval town with curvy paths meandering through
neighborhoods. Some of them are filled with artiasans, such as the shops for building 
superconducting magenets. Restaurant 1 is the town square, the center of social life
for the community, but dotted here and there are the cathedrals. The imposing structures
that house parts of "the machine".

I've walked past one such structure when I've been over at that side of the site; 
[LINAC4](https://cerncourier.com/a/the-long-road-to-linac4/)
<figure>
    <Image src={linac4} alt="The LINAC4" width={600} />
</figure>

Today I decided to figure out what goes on there. LINAC4 takes in the hydrogen ion, H<sup>−</sup> (one proton and two electrons) and
provides the initial acceleration to 160 million electron volts before stripping the 
electrons off and passing the energetic protons into the next step in the injection
chain, the 
[Proton Synchrotron Booster](https://home.cern/science/accelerators/proton-synchrotron-booster/).

Why hydrogen ions? It makes it easier to layer them with the protons already circulating
in the Synchrotron Booster.

How do you strip the electrons off to leave just the proton? Pass the beam through a 
foil made of carbon just a fraction of a micron thick.

The details of just this one part of the LHC serves to show the incredible complexity of 
the machine and what an incredible achievement it is, quietly humming away along the 
boarder of Geneva and France.
