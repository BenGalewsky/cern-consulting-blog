In an earlier post I shared a photo of a building marked "Antimatter
Factory". Many people thought it must be a joke or some sinister James
Bond villain plot. It turns out it is quite real and the truth is much
stranger than our imagination.

A few weeks ago there was a seminar on the workings of the Antimatter Factory.
Naturally I was curious to go along and learn what it really is.

The speaker, Davide Gamba, is a researcher in the Accelerator Beam Physics group at CERN and 
he knows a lot about making antimatter.

Technically known as the Antiproton Deaccelarator (AD), the AD accepts high energy protons from
the [Proton Synchrotron](https://home.cern/science/accelerators/proton-synchrotron/). 
This is the same source of high energy protons that are eventually fed into the Large
Hadron Collider. 

These protons are smashed into an Irridum target. In the spray of resulting particles,
some of them are anti-protons which can be collected by magnetic horns. At this point 
the challenge is to cool these anti-protons down so they can be trapped in a magnetic 
field. Whenever antimatter comes into contact with matter both are immediately inialated. So
this is a tricky matter indeed.

Antielectrons (positrons) come from a radioactive sodium-22 source. These are collected up and 
combined with the anti-protons and are encouraged to form anti-hydrogen.

For many reasons this is the only antimatter factory on the planet. It has been running since 1980 and
it seems unlikely there will be any other such facilities built any time soon.

Earlier, my office mate who has trained as an official CERN guide was taking some visitors around the
site and offered to let me tag along. One stop on our tour was the Antiproton Deaccelarator 
and the Extra Low Energy Antiproton ring.

What do researchers do with Anti-hydrogen? First of all, they trap the atoms in magnetic field 
inside a vacuum. One study has worked to see how long they can keep the atom trapped, apparently
even putting the trap in a van and driving it around the site! In another experiment they deactivated
the magenetic field to see how gravity acts on the anti-matter. It turns out the atom falls
exactly like ordinary hydrogen.

I enjoyed being able to just drop into such a fascinating lecture. A few weeks later I sat 
next to a Hungarian beam engineer at lunch. He supports the Deaccelarator and was very proud
of the unique work they are doing and the research they support.
