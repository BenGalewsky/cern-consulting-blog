Over the coffee kiosk at CERN today there is a sign that reads _C'est la rentrée_.
My trusted French-English dictionary tells me that this phrase means both "the return"
as well as "Septemeber" as in the first day back from holidays, start of school and
the beginning of a new cultural year. In a way, this felt like my first day at school.

# The Magic of EDH
CERN is an enormous enterprise. There are thousands of people who work there on any 
given day. Everything is coordinated by a workflow system called _Electronic Document Handling_
or EDH. Like so much of CERN, this system was developed entirely in-house at the labs.

When all of the stars aligned for me to come on this assignment I had to ask the experiment
Team Leader at the University of Illinois to submit a Home Institution Declaration (HID)
on my behalf to start the process off. Submitting this document caused a review process inside 
CERN that resulted in a Short Stay Invitation along with a French _Convention D’Accueil_ which 
allowed me to apply for my visa.

This same workflow welcomed me on site today and allowed me to sign my contract and get started.
It's a fascinating and baroque process, that handles everything from Payroll to collecitng
my emergency contact info, all triggered by various lifecycle events.

I took the tram out to the main entrance on Esplanade des Particules and walked into the 
community office. All I had to do was show my passport and was immediately given a day pass 
which allows me access to the lab campus.

With my day pass in hand I walked over to the main building and again showed my passport
which allowed the administrator to send an electronic contract to my email. I electroniclly 
signed that and was sent back to the community office where a distracted official took my picture
against the wall with an iPad and printed out my official badge. I'm in!

# R1 Culture
I had some time before meeting with my collaborator. I set up office in the
CERN Restaurant 1 (or R1) - this is very French sort of company canteen. With
very nice food, coffee, and even a wine selection. There are several different spaces
each with different lighting, tables and atmosphere. 

I've been working in this field for eight years now. It was surprising and wonderful
how many people I saw at R1 that I've met at conferences over the years as well
as collaborators in the Software Institute I work with. I was able to share lunch
with a colleague and even plan an outing to this week's Geneva Art Week.

I received orientation into my new projcect. More about that later, but I'll leave you
with the relocated statue of Shiva, a gift from the Indian government to celebrate the
long-standing scientific partnership.

She's now dancing in a car park while construction is under way, but still a marvelous 
sight.
